# The worst century to arrive in, by a wide margin

The Gothic Wars. Rome changes hands repeatedly between 536 and 552 and is besieged three times. In 537, during Vitiges’ siege, the Goths cut the aqueducts. The baths stopped, and so did the water-driven grain mills, which is why Belisarius moored floating mills in the current of the Tiber to keep bread being made at all. The Goths then tried to get in through the dry channel of the Aqua Virgo and were caught.

The system never fully recovered. Popes repaired lines for centuries afterwards, but only the Aqua Virgo, low and mostly underground, ran continuously into the Middle Ages. After Totila took the city in 546, tradition holds Rome stood empty of inhabitants for about forty days: the only interruption in nearly three thousand years.
