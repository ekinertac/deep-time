The Aurelian Wall under construction, 271 CE. Low raking sun, brick-faced concrete, a nineteen-kilometre line being drawn around a city that thought it would never need one.

Begun 271, finished about 275. Roughly 19 km of circuit, about 13.7 km² enclosed. Still standing; breached for the first and last time in 1870.

:::beat after=6 year="537"
# The water stops

The Goths cut the aqueducts during the siege of 537. The baths die, and so do the water mills, and Belisarius moors floating mills in the current of the Tiber to keep bread being made. The system never fully recovers: of the eleven or more lines, only the Aqua Virgo runs continuously into the Middle Ages. This is the single worst century of the twenty, and it has nothing to do with your status.
:::

:::plate after=6 src="img/02-dry-arches.webp"
A dry aqueduct arcade crossing empty country outside the walls, 6th century. Dust, no water, a broken channel, sheep.

Popes repaired individual lines for centuries afterwards. The city that had eleven aqueducts spent the Middle Ages drinking from the river, from wells, and from one surviving line.
:::

:::plate after=8 src="img/03-schola.webp"
The Schola Saxonum in the Borgo, 8th century: a low walled compound of lodging, kitchen and church, with arrivals sleeping in the courtyard.

Traditionally founded 727 by Ine of Wessex. One of four national quarters in the Borgo. The district is still called Sassia and the site is still a hospital.
:::

:::plate after=10 src="img/04-campo-vaccino.webp"
The Roman Forum as pasture: cattle standing among half-buried column drums and the tops of arches, with the ground level metres above the ancient pavement.

The Campo Vaccino, the cow field. Grazed through the Middle Ages, painted by Claude Lorrain in 1636, excavated from 1803.
:::

:::beat after=14 year="1348" quiet=true
# Nothing happens to you

The Black Death reaches Rome in about August 1348 and takes perhaps a third of a city that was already the smallest it had ever been. You walk through it. What is left behind is a labour shortage, which is the only economic condition ever invented that favours a man with no name, no family and no papers.
:::

:::plate after=16 src="img/05-register.webp"
A parish register open on a table, quill, candle, ruled columns of names, ages and households: the stato delle anime.

From the early 17th century every Roman parish wrote down every soul in every house, once a year. The question the city asks a stranger changes here, permanently.
:::

:::beat after=19 year="1871"
# The register becomes the country

A law of 20 June 1871 makes the anagrafe compulsory in every commune in Italy. From here on, existing is an administrative act. The compulsory identity card follows in 1931.
:::
