# The first Jubilee: the city invents the pilgrim crowd

Boniface VIII proclaims a Jubilee in February 1300 and Rome is buried in people. Giovanni Villani, who was there, claims 200,000 pilgrims present at any one time through the year, which historians read as order-of-magnitude at best. Even discounted heavily, a town of thirty thousand absorbed several times itself.

A city that does that has permanent machinery for strangers: hospices, brokers, bread, beds, and the shared assumption that an unexplained foreigner in the street is the normal condition of the place.
