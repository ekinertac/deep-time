# The Grand Tour arrives, with documents

Rome fills with foreigners who cannot speak Italian, which sounds like your best chance since the 15th century. It is not. Those foreigners carry letters of introduction, bankers’ credit and travel papers, and the city has learned to sort the two kinds of stranger from each other at a glance.

In February 1798 the French take Rome, proclaim a republic and deport the pope, who dies in France. Occupations are conducted with lists, and the Napoleonic administration is better at counting people than anything Rome has seen since the empire.
