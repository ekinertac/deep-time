# The city empties, and that is when it becomes survivable

Rome is now a fraction of itself, scattered in patches across a walled area built for an imperial capital. Most of the enclosed thirteen square kilometres becomes the *disabitato*: vineyards, orchards, pasture, ruins and the occasional church. Not slums. Farmland, inside the walls.

The city has also acquired an export and an import. The export is relics and absolution. The import is people who walk in with nothing. The bishop of Rome is now the effective government, and receiving arrivals is not a charitable sideline, it is the institution’s core business.
