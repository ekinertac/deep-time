# Baroque Rome, and a health pass at the gate

The city is spectacular and it is administered. Every resident belongs to a parish and the parish knows their name. Having no fixed abode is a category with a punishment attached, and the great hospices are by now as much workhouse as refuge.

Then the plague of 1656–57 arrives, and Rome answers it with quarantine, cordons and paperwork. A traveller reaching the gates without a *fede di sanità*, a health certificate, is not admitted. It is the first time in this city’s history that a piece of paper is the difference between inside and outside.
