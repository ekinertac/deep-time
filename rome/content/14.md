# The century that kills everyone else hires you

The popes leave for Avignon in 1309 and Rome loses its only industry overnight. The Black Death arrives around August 1348, late, because a city with no pope had little traffic; estimates of mortality run to thirty or forty per cent, with no reliable count. In September 1349 an earthquake brings down the south flank of the Colosseum, and the rubble is quarried for the next three hundred years.

You cannot catch it. You walk through the worst mortality event in European history untouched, into the aftermath, which is a labour shortage. That is the one condition on earth in which a man with no kin, no guild and no claim on anybody can name his price.
