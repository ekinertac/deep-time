# Charity is invented, and so is deportation

Christianity becomes legal, then favoured, and brings an organised duty of care toward strangers as such. The *xenodochium*, a house that receives foreigners, appears as a real institution: Pammachius and Fabiola founded one at Portus, Rome’s harbour, in the 390s.

The same century shows the other face. Ammianus Marcellinus records, with disgust, that when grain ran short Rome expelled the *peregrini*, the foreigners, from the city wholesale, while keeping the dancers and dismissing the teachers. This is the first century in which the city has both instincts at once: a bed for the stranger, and a mechanism for removing him.
