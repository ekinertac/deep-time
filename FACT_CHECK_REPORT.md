# Deep Time fact-check report

**Reviewed:** 30 July 2026  
**Scope:** All 19 HTML pages, their visible captions and claims, plus the values that feed them from `assets/data.js` and `assets/curves.js`. Artistic scene reconstructions were not treated as literal evidence.

## Report card

**Overall BS score: 6/10 — hype-heavy, not fabricated.**

The underlying geologic chronology is generally recognizable and the pages often disclose uncertainty well. The central survival model is much weaker: it turns disputed atmospheric reconstructions, unknown landing conditions, modern nutritional analogies, and unknowable animal behavior into exact countdowns. Several load-bearing rules are wrong, and absolute phrases such as “nothing,” “every,” “only,” and “zero” repeatedly erase known exceptions.

### Bottom line

Keep the site’s narrative framing, but do not present the survival times or the three headline percentages as scientific results. Correct the definite errors below, label the remaining countdowns as scenario-based speculation, and attach a bibliography to the atmospheric curves.

## Definite errors

| Page and claim | Verdict | What the evidence supports |
|---|---|---|
| [`index.html`](index.html):49 — “88% of Earth’s history kills you in under a minute.” | **False; internally contradictory.** | The Precambrian does occupy about 88% of geologic time, but the site’s own data assigns its four entries approximately **1, 2, 3, and 15 minutes** (`assets/data.js`:24–27). A defensible replacement is “about 88% lacks remotely breathable oxygen for an unprotected human.” |
| [`index.html`](index.html):163, [`chart.html`](chart.html):83 and `assets/curves.js`:93 — “You need roughly 16% atmospheric oxygen to stay conscious.” | **False.** | OSHA describes impaired thinking and coordination at 12–16%, faulty judgment and exhaustion at 10–14%, and only “perhaps unconsciousness” at 6–10%. Sixteen percent is hazardous, not a general consciousness cutoff. See [OSHA’s oxygen-deficiency review](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2008-05-01). |
| [`eras/03-proterozoic.html`](eras/03-proterozoic.html):38 — at 20 km, “blood boils at body temperature.” | **False.** | The Armstrong limit applies to **unconfined** water such as saliva and tears. Normal blood pressure prevents a living person’s circulating blood from boiling in vacuum. See NASA’s [*Dressing for Altitude*](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/dressing-for-altitude-ebook_tagged.pdf) and the [NASA space-exposure explanation](https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/ask_astro/space_travel.html). |
| [`eras/04-ediacaran.html`](eras/04-ediacaran.html):27–29, 79 — “No mouths, no guts, no eyes, no shells, no burrows. Nothing here eats anything else.” | **False as a universal claim.** | Ediacaran *Cloudina* was shelly and preserves possible predatory drill holes. Ediacaran trace fossils also record mat-burrowing animals and scavenging. See the reviews of [early drilling predation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28967704/) and [Ediacaran scavenging](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32412628/). “Large macroscopic predation was rare and poorly evidenced” would be safer. |
| [`eras/05-cambrian.html`](eras/05-cambrian.html):61 and [`eras/11-triassic.html`](eras/11-triassic.html):59 — 10% oxygen is compared with roughly 4,000–4,500 m altitude. | **Inaccurate.** | A controlled hypoxia study used **12% O₂** as the normobaric equivalent of about **4,500 m**. Ten percent is substantially more severe, so the pages understate the low end of their own oxygen ranges. See [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-26116-y). |
| [`eras/08-devonian.html`](eras/08-devonian.html):79, 158 — roots made rivers start to meander; these were “the first” clear meandering rivers. | **False.** | Meandering does not require vegetation. Pre-vegetation meandering systems are documented as far back as the Proterozoic; plants mainly stabilized banks and slowed migration. See [Nature Geoscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-019-0491-7). |
| [`eras/12-jurassic.html`](eras/12-jurassic.html):8, 108, 128, 156, 161 — no Jurassic plant was edible; every plant was indigestible or toxic. [`eras/13-cretaceous.html`](eras/13-cretaceous.html):33 then calls flowering plants the first edible land food. | **False and internally contradictory.** | The Jurassic page itself says ginkgo seeds are edible in small quantities. More broadly, gymnosperms are a 300-million-year-old lineage that includes edible seeds, and some fern shoots are edible when properly prepared. That does **not** prove a random Jurassic species was safe, but it defeats the universal “none anywhere” claim. See the [Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh](https://www.rbge.org.uk/science-and-conservation/preserved-collections/herbarium/our-collections/gymnosperms/) and [Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-safety-fruits-vegetables/fiddlehead-safety-tips.html). Replace with “no reliably identifiable staple without species knowledge and processing.” |
| [`eras/14-paleogene.html`](eras/14-paleogene.html):28 — “The dinosaurs are gone.” | **Taxonomically false.** | Non-avian dinosaurs were gone; birds survived and are dinosaurs. See the [Smithsonian](https://www.si.edu/stories/could-dinosaurs-still-exist) and its [K–Pg extinction summary](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/education/teaching-resources/paleontology/extinction-over-time). |
| [`eras/16-quaternary.html`](eras/16-quaternary.html):81 — the Quaternary is summarized as repeated cycles of roughly 90,000 icy years and 10,000 warm years. | **False for much of the period.** | Before the Mid-Pleistocene Transition, glacial cycles were paced near 41,000 years; the stronger ~100,000-year pattern emerged roughly one million years ago. See the [U.S. National Science Foundation](https://www.nsf.gov/news/why-did-glacial-cycles-intensify-million-years-ago). |
| [`eras/16-quaternary.html`](eras/16-quaternary.html):160 — “glacial meltwater is as clean as water gets.” | **False and unsafe as practical advice.** | Clear meltwater can carry microbes or harmful chemicals. CDC guidance is to treat water whenever its safety is unknown and explicitly warns that clear mountain water can be unsafe. See the [CDC water-treatment guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/drinking-water/prevention/water-treatment-hiking-camping-traveling.html). |
| Site-wide navigation and headings — all 16 entries are called “eras.” | **Incorrect terminology.** | The list mixes eons (Hadean, Archean, Proterozoic), periods (Cambrian through Quaternary), and one period split out from its parent eon (Ediacaran). “Intervals” or “chapters” would be accurate. The formal hierarchy is eon → era → period → epoch; see the [National Park Service geologic-timescale guide](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/grcatime-timescale.htm). |

## Misleading, overstated, or unsupported claims

| Page and claim | Verdict | Why it needs revision |
|---|---|---|
| [`index.html`](index.html):171 and repeated era pages — ancient pathogens “have no idea what to do with your immune system,” so you cannot catch disease. | **Misleading.** | Host adaptation matters, but it is not an immunity guarantee. Host jumps occur, and free-living environmental organisms can infect humans without a co-evolved human host. The site partly concedes this for amoebae and wounds, contradicting its categorical wording. See the [CDC on free-living amoebic infections](https://www.cdc.gov/dpdx/freelivingamebic/index.html). Correct wording: “many host-specific pathogens would probably fail, but infection risk is unknowable and environmental opportunists remain possible.” |
| [`index.html`](index.html):167 and [`eras/05-cambrian.html`](eras/05-cambrian.html):141 — lean protein becomes fatal “once protein passes about 35% of calories.” | **Misleading threshold.** | Thirty-five percent is the upper end of the adult Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range, not an established cliff at which death begins. The National Academies also says no defined intake level for adverse effects was identified. “Rabbit starvation” depends on total energy, fat, duration, individual physiology, and the actual food. See the [National Academies DRI summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/cdn/materials/9fb9fae1-63a0-4048-88ad-3f972639149a). |
| [`eras/01-hadean.html`](eras/01-hadean.html):76–96 — “up to 100 bar” CO₂ and 200°C+ are displayed like era-wide conditions. | **Misleading presentation.** | The page’s own caveat correctly calls 100 bar an early-Hadean upper bound across a two-order-of-magnitude uncertainty. Zircons indicate liquid water and possibly oceans by about 4.4 Ga. The vitals should be split into early and late Hadean scenarios instead of showing one extreme snapshot for 509 million years. See [NASA on the ancient zircon evidence](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/ancient-crystals-suggest-earlier-ocean/). |
| [`eras/05-cambrian.html`](eras/05-cambrian.html):28 — “Every major animal body plan alive today appears in this window.” | **Overstated.** | The Cambrian records most major animal phyla and a dramatic radiation, but many fossils are stem lineages rather than modern crown groups, and some divergences predate the Cambrian. “Most major animal phyla enter the fossil record” is the defensible formulation. See this [evolutionary review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3861885/). |
| [`eras/09-carboniferous.html`](eras/09-carboniferous.html):63 — the Carboniferous was “the only era where the air is richer than yours.” | **Internally inconsistent and too absolute.** | The Permian page says its oxygen starts near the Carboniferous peak, so the site’s own ranges imply part of the Permian also exceeded modern oxygen. Paleo-oxygen values are model ranges, not measurements; the page already acknowledges that 25–35% estimates disagree. |
| [`eras/10-permian.html`](eras/10-permian.html):30–31, 95, 122 and similar predator passages — particular extinct animals would deliberately hunt a human, while Jurassic theropods would be merely curious. | **Unverifiable.** | Body size, feeding anatomy, and ecology can inform risk; intent and a species’ response to an unprecedented primate cannot be recovered from fossils. These passages should be labeled speculative scenarios, not comparative facts. |
| [`eras/13-cretaceous.html`](eras/13-cretaceous.html):153 — Chicxulub re-entry made the sky a broiler across much of the planet; outdoors on the “wrong hemisphere” meant minutes. | **Disputed model presented as settled.** | Modeling finds strongly nonuniform thermal radiation: some downrange regions may have ignited, while many uprange and distal sites received pulses too weak for immediate ignition. See [Morgan, Artemieva & Goldin (2013)](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2013JG002428). The global “broiler” should be framed as one debated scenario. |
| [`eras/15-neogene.html`](eras/15-neogene.html):63–65 — Neogene CO₂ was 280–400 ppm and “at or slightly below” pre-industrial. | **Misleading and partly self-contradictory.** | Pre-industrial CO₂ was about 280 ppm, so 400 ppm is not “at or below” it. Recent reconstructions converge around 400–600 ppm for the early Miocene and Miocene Climatic Optimum, with some higher estimates. See the [2021 Miocene review](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2020PA004037). Use a time-varying range rather than one value for 20 million years. |
| All verdict pages and [`index.html`](index.html):41, 48–60 — exact survival times and “1.5% normal life.” | **Unverifiable, with false precision.** | No transparent model or cited experiment maps reconstructed paleo-atmospheres and ecosystems to these countdowns. Outcomes depend on the exact date and location within intervals lasting millions to billions of years, atmospheric pressure, season, weather, health, acclimatization, skill, prey composition, and luck. The verdicts are useful thought experiments, but they are not measured or reproducible estimates. |

## Stale values

- `chart.html`:112, `assets/curves.js`:38, 50, 106, and several era pages use “today ~425 ppm.” NOAA’s latest complete global monthly mean available during this review was **428.55 ppm for April 2026**. Use a dated value or load current data rather than hard-coding “today.” See [NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory](https://www.gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/global.html).
- The footer says dates follow the ICS 2023 chart. That disclosure is fair, and the boundaries largely track the cited edition, but it should say **“rounded from ICS 2023”** because the site starts at Earth’s approximate formation age (4,540 Ma) rather than the chart’s 4,567 Ma lower limit and uses custom grouping. See the [ICS 2023 chart](https://stratigraphy.org/ICSchart/ChronostratChart2023-09.pdf).

## What held up

- The claim that the Precambrian represents about 88% of geologic time is sound; the error is attaching “under a minute” to all four displayed entries.
- A roughly 16% oxygen threshold for **sustained fire** is defensible. The site incorrectly turns that combustion threshold into a human consciousness threshold.
- The broad order of geologic intervals and most rounded boundary dates align with the disclosed ICS 2023 source.
- The pages frequently acknowledge uncertainty in oxygen reconstructions, animal behavior, Hadean conditions, and Snowball Earth. Those caveats are valuable; the main text should use the same restraint.

## Highest-priority edits

1. Replace the 16% “consciousness threshold” everywhere and decouple it from the fire threshold.
2. Change exact survival verdicts to scenario ranges or clearly label them as speculative storytelling.
3. Correct the Ediacaran, Devonian rivers, Jurassic/Cretaceous food, Paleogene dinosaur, and Quaternary water/cycle claims.
4. Remove universal pathogen immunity and the 35%-protein death threshold.
5. Rename the 16 “eras” to “intervals,” add a bibliography for every curve, and date all “today” comparisons.

## Hype and incentive check

The recurring hype pattern is **absolute language plus false precision**: “every,” “nothing,” “only,” “exact,” minute-by-minute death clocks, and global percentages built from those clocks. Several caveat sections then soften claims made categorically above them.

No commercial funnel, sponsorship, or sales incentive was found in the reviewed pages. The apparent incentive is narrative impact and memorability, not financial conversion. That is why this report rates the site as hype-heavy rather than deceptive.
